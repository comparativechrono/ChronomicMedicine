    What is genomic medicine?

    Using a patient’s whole genome sequence to find the cause of their disease

    • 2018The NHS Genomic Medicine Service launched. The NHS was the first national health system to offer whole genome sequencing as part of routine care.
    • 100,000genomes were sequenced from about 85,000 NHS patients with rare disease or cancer for the 100,000 Genomes Project.
    • 1 in 17people will have a rare disease at some point in their life, and around 80% of rare diseases have a genetic cause.

    Sources for this screen

    1. 100,000 whole genomes sequenced through the NHS Genomic Medicine ServiceGenomics England news, August 2024. “The NHS is the first national health system in the world to offer whole genome sequencing” as part of routine care.
    2. The 100,000 Genomes ProjectGenomics England. Genomes from around 85,000 NHS patients affected by rare disease or cancer; 100,000 genomes sequenced by December 2018.
    3. The UK Rare Diseases FrameworkDepartment of Health and Social Care, 2021. 1 in 17 people affected by a rare disease in their lifetime; over 7,000 rare diseases; 80% with an identified genetic origin.

    The NHS genomic medicine service is here! Patients with rare diseases are now offered the opportunity to have whole genome sequencing performed on the NHS to identify a causal variant if standard testing fails. Only 30% of cases from the NHS genomic medicine service are currently solved. We need researchers to help us solve the many individuals who have been sequenced.

    You will work in the Linux computing environment to examine potential causal variants in a set of patients, and feed your findings back to the NHS. This project has the potential to lead directly to improvements in a patient’s life.

    Chronomic medicine

    Your DNA doesn’t change over the day, but your gene activity does

    52% of disease-causing genes are predicted to oscillate across the day. We call the application of chronobiology to genomic medicine chronomic medicine.

    • SeverityThe clock may help explain why the same variant is mild in one person and severe in another.
    • DiagnosisVariants in the DNA that controls when genes are switched on could be missed causes of disease.
    • TreatmentThe time of day a medicine is taken can change how well it works.

    Sources for this screen

    The 52% figure is our group’s estimate of disease-causing genes predicted to oscillate across the day. Times on the dial are typical of a day-active adult and vary from person to person: core body temperature is lowest a couple of hours before waking, cortisol peaks shortly after waking, heart attacks are most common between 6 am and noon, and melatonin starts to rise two to three hours before bedtime.

    1. Role of the circadian system in cardiovascular diseaseThosar SS, Butler MP, Shea SA (2018) Journal of Clinical Investigation 128:2157–2167. Includes the morning peak in heart attacks and the blood pressure surge after waking.

    3. Central and peripheral circadian clocks in mammalsMohawk JA, Green CB, Takahashi JS (2012) Annual Review of Neuroscience 35:445–462

    The same disease variant can be severe in one person and mild in another. You will test whether genetic variants that make people morning or evening types help to explain the difference.

    With Tim Hearn and Millie Ampiah

    Is circadian disruption of rhythmically expressed genes behind missing heritability in rare disease?

    • Computational project
    • 1 student
    • Dry lab
    • Addenbrooke’s Treatment Centre

    You will continue an existing study looking for associations between variants influencing genetic chronotype, the genetic basis for “morning” and “evening” people, and rare disease phenotype severity. We have previously identified loci that affect the severity of cardiac arrhythmias and you will continue this work with other candidate diseases.

    There are many common endocrine disorders where the genetic aetiology is complicated and there is contribution from a variety of genes in related pathways to the overall disease phenotype. Endocrine disorders are excellent candidates for chronomic medicine, as the disease genes are highly differentially expressed. You will ascertain the number of genes on the endocrine disorders gene panel which are rhythmically expressed in patient samples. You will then perform an analysis on the patient genomes in the 100,000 Genomes Project endocrine disorders group.

    It is possible to conduct this project on any other rare disease or cancer of interest to a student.

    Practical details

    • The work uses the Genomics England 100,000 Genomes Project data, inside the secure Genomics England Research Environment.
    • You will sign up to the Genomics England clinical interpretation partnership agreement and complete information governance training.

    Almost every cell in the body has its own circadian clock. You will synchronise cultured human cells with a brief hormone pulse and measure when the clock genes are expressed across the day.

    With Tim Hearn

    Timing of human cell clock

    • Lab project
    • Human cell culture

    Our body clock runs inside individual cells, where clock-gene expression rises and falls at characteristic times of the day. A brief hormonal pulse can synchronize cultured cells so that their clocks run in phase, allowing us to study these rhythms under controlled experimental conditions.

    This project will characterize clock-gene activities in human cells across the day, using a range of experimental techniques, to better understand the biology of circadian rhythms.

    Developing and benchmarking Native Workbench: accessible, reproducible bioinformatics on Windows

    • Computational project
    • Software and benchmarking

    Running bioinformatics analyses can involve complex software setup, creating barriers for students and researchers. Native Workbench is a Windows desktop application designed to make established analysis tools easier to run locally while recording how results were produced.

    This project will develop the application further and evaluate its scientific correctness, performance, usability and reproducibility. The student will test workflows such as sequence quality control, alignment and variant calling using datasets with known expected results and larger, realistic datasets. Scientific outputs, execution times and computing-resource use will be compared with equivalent Linux analyses under matched conditions wherever practical.

    Testing across different Windows computers and structured user feedback will guide improvements to installation, workflow construction and error recovery. The project will deliver an improved application and a reproducible benchmark suite, alongside evidence-based guidance on its capabilities and limitations.

    Practical details

    • A good understanding of the use of AI agents and GitHub would be an advantage.
